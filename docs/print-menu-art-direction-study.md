# Haven Print Menu: Editorial Art Direction Study

## Purpose

Create an A4 bilingual menu that feels like a collectible independent publication while still helping a customer choose quickly and desire the products. The design should be premium through restraint, material quality, product truth, and rhythm rather than through conventional luxury decoration.

## Reference Families

### Coffee and independent magazines

- **Drift** treats coffee as culture and place, combining original photography, long-form pacing, and a large-format print object. Its lesson for Haven is that the cup does not always need to fill the frame; place, ritual, hands, surfaces, and light can make coffee feel meaningful.
- **Standart** explicitly positions itself around original photography and considered design made to be kept rather than scrolled past. Its lesson is durability: typography and images should reward a second look, not behave like disposable advertising.
- **Kinfolk** uses calm framing, controlled palettes, and deliberate negative space. The useful lesson is visual silence around an object. The danger is making food too pale, remote, or bloodless.
- **Apartamento** offers the counterpoint: lived-in, specific, human photography rather than immaculate styling. Its lesson is credibility. A wrinkle, crumb, used spoon, or imperfect shadow can make a scene feel inhabited and desirable.

### Contemporary hospitality design

- Award-winning hospitality environments increasingly use a materially restrained but expressive language: stone, plaster, texture, muted fields, and a small number of distinctive gestures.
- Strong menu systems respond to the physical operation. Loose or replaceable sheets can feel premium when paired with a substantial reusable cover, and are more practical for changing prices than a permanently bound object.
- Recent bilingual menu work commonly succeeds by separating content clearly while maintaining one visual identity, using disciplined hierarchy rather than decorating both languages differently.

## What Beautiful Minimal Layouts Have in Common

### 1. One dominant gesture per spread

A spread usually has one clear event: a full-bleed photograph, a small isolated object, an oversized title, or a disciplined column of text. It does not ask typography, photography, ornament, and colour to compete at equal volume.

**Haven rule:** each page gets one protagonist. Menu listings remain quiet; signature pages may become visually expressive.

### 2. Rhythmic contrast, not uniform prettiness

The strongest publications alternate density and emptiness. A nearly blank cover makes the first product image feel richer. A dense price page makes the following full-page image feel luxurious. Repeating the same grid and image size on every page creates a catalogue rather than a publication.

**Haven rule:** alternate quiet, image-led, and information-led pages. Never place three equal hero images in a row.

### 3. Large margins act as material

Whitespace is not simply unused paper. It creates pause, frames the texture of the stock, and signals confidence. Premium layouts tend to use wider outer margins than expected and align content to a small number of invisible vertical lines.

**Haven rule:** begin with 18 mm outer margins, 22 mm at the binding edge, and a restrained six-column grid. Let at least one-third of selected pages remain visually unoccupied.

### 4. Typography behaves like architecture

Elegant serif display type is effective when used at meaningful scale and with ordinary letter spacing. Supporting information is quieter and more systematic. The best designs use few type sizes but create contrast through scale, weight, placement, and white space.

**Haven rule:** use Cormorant Garamond for English display language, not every menu line. Pair it with a highly readable text face and an Arabic face with equivalent colour and dignity. Prices align on a fixed axis and never float unpredictably.

### 5. Captions create intimacy

Independent magazines often use very small captions or marginal notes. These can make an image feel observed rather than advertised.

**Haven rule:** use short product-proof notes such as "Haven recipe / passion fruit / cold brewed coffee" rather than generic promotional claims.

### 6. Imperfection is edited, not erased

Apartamento-like realism and premium still life meet in controlled naturalism: real condensation, a slight drip, crumbs, foam texture, the true edge of ice. Removing every irregularity makes food look synthetic.

**Haven rule:** preserve product-specific irregularities while removing distractions that do not belong to the product story.

## The Photographic Grammar of Appetite

### Product truth first

The actual cup, logo, portion, liquid colour, ice, garnish, and serving vessel must remain unchanged. AI may create or refine a set, but the product layer should come from a real photograph and remain traceable to it.

### Focus and softness

- Keep the decision-making evidence sharp: the front rim, top surface, foam, ice, cake crumb, melting edge, or condensation.
- Allow softness to begin behind the product rather than across it.
- Use shallow focus for intimacy, not to hide half the drink.
- A full product portrait should retain enough depth of field that the customer understands the portion and texture.

### Colour

- Preserve believable coffee, milk, matcha, karkade, fruit, and chocolate colour.
- Moderate saturation supports appetite; desaturation weakens perceived palatability and craving.
- Concentrate colour in the product while keeping the environment quieter.
- Use Haven red as a small visual echo, not as a global colour cast.
- Avoid heavy orange grading, which makes cream, milk, paper, and skin look unclean.

### Light

- Use directional light from roughly 45 degrees to reveal foam, crumb, ice edges, and condensation.
- Keep highlights bright enough to communicate freshness but below clipped white.
- A soft shadow gives weight; a black theatrical shadow makes a daytime cafe product feel remote.
- For Salalah, clear morning or late-afternoon light is more authentic than anonymous studio darkness.

### Composition

- Crop close enough to trigger imagined texture, but retain one image per section that proves the complete portion.
- Use negative space intentionally for headings; do not create empty space that leads the eye away from the product.
- Place circular cups and plates slightly away from dead centre. Let rims, spoons, cloth folds, and shadows guide the eye back to the product.
- Use foreground blur rarely and only when it creates presence. Never obscure the product with decorative objects.
- Avoid the repeated three-quarter tabletop shot. Alternate eye level, near-overhead, macro detail, and human-scale ritual.

## Four Haven Image Types

### 1. The product portrait

Complete drink or dessert, truthful portion, bright directional light, restrained surface, high detail. Used for Spanish Latte and Matcha.

### 2. The sensory detail

Macro view of condensation, coffee meeting milk, brownie crumb, or affogato melt. Used as a narrow crop or full-page interruption, paired with a complete product image elsewhere.

### 3. The ritual

Espresso being poured over vanilla ice cream, a V60 pour, or a cup being placed on the table. Hands may appear, but the action and product remain central. This communicates professional knowledge without publishing recipes.

### 4. The Haven setting

A real cup in recognisable Salalah light with one local material cue. The setting should suggest place without becoming tourism imagery.

## Proposed A4 Sequence

1. **Cover:** warm white field; silver Haven mark; small "Osara, Salalah"; no photograph.
2. **Invitation:** one signature image with a short bilingual line about the founding barista and Haven's approach to flavour.
3. **Haven signatures:** Spanish Latte, Haven Passion Fruit Cold Brew, Matcha, and one dessert. One dominant photograph plus concise product proof.
4. **Hot coffee:** structured listings with one small ritual photograph and a restrained taste-navigation key.
5. **Iced drinks:** brighter image, more colour, and cold sensory cues. Keep the listing highly scannable.
6. **Tea and specials:** lighter editorial page with Karkade as the visual anchor.
7. **Sweets and desserts:** one vivid close photograph, more tactile cropping, minimal copy.
8. **Back page:** quiet brand close, location, social details, and QR.

An eight-page edition is preferable if the menu is bound or produced as a four-sheet double-sided set. A six-page edition remains possible for a replaceable menu-cover system, but images will need to share more space with listings.

## Appetite Without Visual Noise

Professional food photography can improve consumption vision and attitudes toward a cafe menu, but adding a photograph to every item erodes distinction and can increase cognitive load. Haven should photograph only products that carry one of three jobs:

1. Prove a signature or unique recipe.
2. Make an unfamiliar product immediately understandable.
3. Create a strong sensory transition between menu sections.

The image must answer at least one customer question: "How indulgent is it?", "How cold is it?", "How sweet is it?", "What texture will I experience?", or "How large is it?"

## Initial Shot List

1. Spanish Latte: full product portrait, milk-coffee transition clearly visible.
2. Haven Passion Fruit Cold Brew: complete serving plus macro condensation detail; passion fruit presence must match service.
3. Matcha: vivid but believable green, ice edges and milk movement, no neon grading.
4. Affogato: real pour sequence, premium vanilla ice cream and actual espresso used by Haven.
5. Brownie or San Sebastian: cut surface showing true crumb and moisture.
6. V60: barista ritual with hands, brewer, and controlled stream; no recipe quantities visible.
7. Karkade: translucent ruby colour against bright daylight, with the actual garnish and glass.

## Production Safeguards

- Keep the original capture and final composite side by side for approval.
- Record what was changed in every AI-assisted image.
- Never generate or alter the Haven logo inside an image; place the vector logo in layout or preserve the photographed mark.
- Proof at actual A4 size, not only on screen.
- Print one colour proof on the intended 180gsm stock before the full run.
- Use the printer's CMYK profile and confirm whether metallic silver means foil, metallic spot ink, or a cool-grey simulation.
